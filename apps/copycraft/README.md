# CopyCraft

A clipboard command for macOS. Press **⌃⌘C** and a card opens at the cursor; paste (⌘V) or drop something on it and the card shows it, plus the actions that fit it. By default CopyCraft does not read the clipboard on its own (paste-first); with *Keep clipboard history* on, the card shows what you copied. The menu bar icon shows that CopyCraft is running: a monochrome template that follows the menu bar (light, dark or tinted) and, with history on, blinks briefly on each new copy. The detected kind (JSON, Rust, Image, a link's host, YouTube, …) shows in its tooltip and its VoiceOver label. The app has no Dock icon.

Part of the [mac-apps](../../README.md) monorepo.

## Features

- **Paste-first, history opt-in**: *Keep clipboard history* (*Klembordgeschiedenis bijhouden*; on the welcome screen and at the top of Settings › Privacy, each with one sentence on what it does) is **off on a new install**. Off, CopyCraft never polls the pasteboard (not even its change count) and does not read it when the card opens; ⌘V in the card reads it once, because you asked, and a drop works as always. What you paste or drop goes on the card with every tool (decoders, table, diff, *Text ▾*, AI) and into the in-memory history through `sensitivity::admission` (a token or key stays out; a copy another app marks private shows *Hidden content* and is not kept). An empty card says *Paste here (⌘V) or turn on history in Settings*. *Pause Recording*, *Ignore Next Copy* and *Ignored apps* only apply to watching, so they are greyed out while it is off. On: everything below about polling and reading copies applies. An install that already had settings (or had closed the welcome screen) when this setting arrived keeps it **on**; the choice is stored at the first launch of that build (`settings::settle_clipboard_history`, pure rule in `src/history_mode.rs` with unit tests). Turning it off keeps what is already in history (use *Clear History* or *Wipe*), resets *Pause* and *Ignore Next Copy* and zeroizes the pasteboard read caches. ⌘V with the caret in a search, find or filter field pastes into that field (through the hidden Edit menu); elsewhere it pastes onto the card.

- **Detection**: JSON, YAML, XML, HTML, Markdown, Rust, Java, Python, URLs, CSV/TSV, plain text and images. JSON and XML get a *Valid* / *Invalid* title. The card header shows only the kind (*Rust*, *JSON*): a check after it, such as *missing }* or *Invalid*, leads the meta line, and the hotkey is in the ✕ tooltip (*Close (Esc, ⌃⌘C)*). Text that starts with `{` or `[` and is meant as JSON (a quoted key such as `"name":` or `'name':`, or an array that opens with a string) but does not parse is *JSON · Invalid*: it stays as copied (no YAML reading, even when YAML would accept a trailing comma or single quotes), the meta line says where (*Line 4, column 1: trailing comma*) and the well marks that line. `{a: 1}` without quotes, and JSON lines (one object per line), are not taken for broken JSON. A page with `<!DOCTYPE html>` or a root `<html>` is *HTML* (also on one line, where a style such as `color: teal` once made it read as YAML): highlighted and formatted, without the XML check or a Schema chip. Rust and Java whose brackets do not balance (strings, char literals and comments ignored) get a title such as *Java · missing }* or *Rust · unbalanced )*; formatting still runs and adds no brackets. Python is recognised by a score of Python-only hints (`def`/`class …:`, `import`, `from … import`, `elif`, `with open(`, `self`, `None`/`True`/`False`, an indented block after `:`, `print(`, f-strings, `#` comments) with at least one strong hint, so a lone line such as `f = open("demofile.txt")` stays plain text; Ruby, JavaScript, YAML and prose do not qualify. Python is highlighted but not reformatted, and its title flags brackets (*Python · missing )*), a block without an indented body (*Python · expected indent*) and *mixed tabs and spaces*.
- **Format**: pretty-print with syntax highlighting. JSON, YAML, XML, HTML, Markdown, Rust and Java open already formatted. Formatting is built in and runs in the app, with no outside tools: Rust and Java are re-indented by their brackets (strings, char literals and comments ignored, multi-line strings and comments kept as they are), not run through `rustfmt` or any other program. HTML has its own layout, apart from XML: void elements (`<meta>`, `<link>`, `<br>`, `<img>`, `<hr>`, `<input>`, …) do not open a level, the contents of `script`, `style`, `pre` and `textarea` stay exactly as copied, text with inline elements (`<p>Some <em>words</em>.<br>More.</p>`) stays on one line with its spaces, and a left-out end tag (`<li>`, `<p>`, `<td>`) does not make the indentation drift. XML formatting is unchanged.
- **To JSON**: a YAML entry gets a *To JSON* chip: the YAML as pretty JSON, as a view of the entry (Copy and Save take it, saved as `.json`). Anchors and aliases are expanded (`<<` merge keys too), keys such as `1:` or `true:` become strings, dates stay the text they were copied as, and a copy of several documents (`---`) becomes a JSON array. YAML that does not parse gets no chip, and JSON (which is also YAML) never does. The view gets its own sensitivity labels and is masked like the copy.
- **Convert**: flat `key: value` text → JSON (the *Convert* chip). JSON, CSV and TSV have no conversion: JSON opens formatted, and a CSV or TSV stays the copied text on the card. *Open Table* opens its grid; Save there writes CSV with the source's separator.
- **Decoders**: one chip, titled with what the copy decodes as (*JWT*, *UUID*, *Unix time* / *Unix-tijd*, *ISO 8601*, *URL*, *Hash*, *Base64*), shows the decoded text read-only; clicking that chip again goes back to the card's own view. See [Decoders](#decoders).
- **Table**: a CSV or TSV opens on the card as the copied text, aligned and colored as that table. The only chip is *Open Table* (it opens a window). There is no *Dataframe* chip, no *Table ▾* chip and no *Show Columns* / *Show Table* chip. JSON opens formatted (with *Schema* when it parses) and is not offered as a table. *Open Table* closes the card and opens the grid in its own window (see *Table versions*). Saving from that window writes CSV of the version shown (UTF-8, the source's separator (comma, `;` or tab), a header with the real column names, dates as `yyyy-mm-dd`, quoted where needed), named *people.csv* after *people.tsv* or *clipboard.csv*; the save panel's *Format* popup (CSV / Parquet) can pick Parquet instead, and switches the name's extension. An export with up to 10 lines of text above its header (a title, a *Definition: …* line) is still a table: those lines are skipped for the table, its labels and its exports, the meta line says *Header on line N*, and the card shows them as copied above the aligned table. Columns of ISO dates (`2026-03-09`) and datetimes (`2026-03-09T14:05`, also with a space, seconds or a fraction; no time zone) are read as dates and datetimes when every value is one (one other value keeps the column text), so a CSV saved from the table reads back typed. Columns of `dd/mm/yyyy` text (also with `-` or `.`) are read as dates, so a saved CSV has `yyyy-mm-dd` dates and Parquet a date column; a value with a second part over 12 makes it `mm/dd/yyyy`. When every day and month is 12 or less, both orders fit: a new table starts from Settings › Date order (day first unless you chose `mm/dd`) and the meta line says *Dates read as dd/mm/yyyy* or *Dates read as mm/dd/yyyy*. When a value only fits month first (its second part is over 12, as in `01/13/2026`), the column is read `mm/dd/yyyy` and the meta line says *Dates read as mm/dd/yyyy*, without a chip to switch. A column with values that only fit day first and others that only fit month first stays text. Numbers stored as text (quoted, or with a decimal comma as in `2,50`) are typed as the table is read, when every value of the column (empty ones aside) converts; a column with one other value stays text. This happens before the original version, without a step or a note. A leading zero (`007`, `0612345678`) keeps the column text. Copy and Save on the card take CSV, never the aligned text. The window's meta line says *N rows × M columns*. Where 3 or more column names share a prefix of 10 characters or more up to a separator (` - `, `: `, ` | `, …), the grid and the sidebar show it as *…* (*… PV1 Generation*), per group; Copy and Save keep the real names.
- **No Raw or Original chip**: a text card always shows its formatted view (formatted JSON, XML, YAML, TOML, code, …); there is no chip back to the copy as it came. The chip of the view shown (*Schema*, *To JSON*, a decoder, *Diff*) clicked again goes back to the card's own view. After text recognition a picture card has one toggle, *Show Text* / *Show Image* (*Toon tekst* / *Toon afbeelding*), named after where it goes; otherwise its active chip (*Info*, *Text*, *QR*) clicked again shows the picture, and ↶ goes back to v1. The title says *Formatted …* only when formatting changed the text.
- **Table versions**: *Open Table* opens the one table window, titled *Table* (*Tabel*), or the file's name when the table comes from a chosen file. Its toolbar is a real macOS toolbar in the title bar (unified compact, not customisable, no Customize Toolbar…): *Table ▾* (a menu item, the one text button), *Copy* and *Save* as symbols only (`doc.on.doc`, `square.and.arrow.down`; what they do in the tooltip, their name for VoiceOver and in the » overflow menu of a narrow window), the hint while masked, and the version group at the right. *Table ▾* has exactly *Remove Duplicate Rows* (keeps the first), *Remove Empty Rows and Columns* (trims text first), *Choose Columns…*, *Sort Ascending ›* and *Sort Descending ›* (per column; empty cells last, equal values keep their order). Group by, Filter and Join are not there: they were removed on purpose, and a table card and the table window have no AI chip either. The same steps are in command search on the card (*Undo Table Step* and *Redo Table Step* too). Undo and redo are ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z. A step started from the card shows that version's grid on the card; clicking its chip again goes back to the aligned text. While that grid is showing, the chip row has the version capsule once there is more than one version, and, when a date column fits both orders, a chip *Dates are mm/dd/yyyy* (and back). Reading the table another way starts its versions over from the original, as its columns can change. A step adds a version to the entry instead of a new entry: the original, then one per step, at most 20. The card and the window share that list. The capsule is undo ↶, the version shown (*v2/3*; click it for the list, the one shown checked) and redo ↷. On the card it sits at the right end of the chip row. On the window it is the toolbar's version group (↶, the version shown with its list, ↷); the meta line (rows × columns, labels, the step) is along the bottom. The step of the version shown is also in the meta line (*· Duplicates removed*). A step taken from an older version drops the versions after it. Steps run on a background thread (the spinner shows when slow; a newer step cancels the one running). The table is read in the background as soon as the card shows a CSV or TSV, so the menu knows its columns. *Choose Columns…* on the window is a dialog: a checkbox per column (the shortened name, the real one in its tooltip), *All* / *None*, and *Apply* or *Cancel*. Apply needs at least one column (otherwise it beeps); with every column still checked it just closes. The same command from the card's search opens a picker over the well: a filter field, a checkbox per column (the shortened name, the real one in its tooltip, the type dimmed), *All* / *None* (on the columns the filter shows; ⌘A is *All*), *Keeping 8 of 20*, and *Apply* (Return) or *Cancel* (Esc). Space ticks the focused checkbox, ↑ ↓ move through the list. *Apply* is off only while no column is kept. *Apply* takes one step, *Kept 8 of 20 columns*, in the table's column order when the set changed; with every column still checked it just closes. *Cancel*, Esc and a click outside the panel dismiss it. The card picker also closes when the app loses focus, on Wipe, lock, retention and when the entry or version changes. Neither picker shows cell values. There is no column overview of sample values: the window's sidebar lists each column's name with its type dimmed beside it, and the toolbar's *Columns* button hides the sidebar. In the grid the first column stays in view while you scroll sideways, as a frozen column in a spreadsheet (from two columns up), in the window and on the card while the card shows the grid: it scrolls up and down with the rows, is drawn from the grid's own text (so it is blurred, masked and wiped with the well) and is left out for VoiceOver, which reads the grid itself. The window shows every row of the version; only tables above 100,000 rows are truncated, with *Showing N of M rows* in the meta. ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z undo and redo there, ⌘W closes it and ⌘M minimises it. Opening another table replaces the window. It opens with the table visible; with the privacy filter on it blurs again when it loses focus (a click reveals it; with it off the table is always shown directly); without a blur filter it stays blank under a hint until revealed; sidebar, grid and frozen column are left out for VoiceOver; it is closed and its text wiped on Wipe, lock, sleep and retention, and whenever its entry leaves the history (*Clear History* leaves a pinned entry's window open) and when the chosen file it shows leaves the card. Copy and Save on the window, and on the card while it shows a table version, take the full version shown: CSV on Copy (the source's separator, `yyyy-mm-dd` dates; the drawn grid is for the screen only), CSV or Parquet on Save. A right-click (or ⌃-click) on a cell of the aligned grid opens *Copy Cell*, *Copy Row* (tab-separated) and *Copy Column* (one value per line, without its name), from the version's own values, not the clipped text on screen; each is a new copy in history, as *Copy* is. A masked grid, and the boxed grid that does not parse, keep the text view's own menu. Moving between versions (↶ ↷, the capsule, ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z) only shows them and writes nothing to the clipboard. Its size and sensitivity labels are the version's own. Content you revealed stays revealed in every view, chip, version and step of the entry, also when a step finishes or its labels arrive; another entry, focus loss, Wipe, lock and retention mask it again. Only the steps are kept for every version; the original and the shown table are kept as frames for the entry on the card and those near it, and are worked out again from the copied text when needed. Versions go with their entry (Wipe, lock, retention, and *Clear History* when the entry is not pinned). No new entitlements.
- **Glass and welcome screen**: windows use the Liquid Glass effect (macOS 26); only the welcome screen is opaque navy (the icon's colour, always dark appearance). The welcome screen opens once, on first launch, and can be reopened from the menu bar menu with *Welcome Screen* (or the card's `⋯` menu). It lists the hotkey (*Open CopyCraft from any app with ⌃⌘C.*, showing the hotkey currently set), that the history is kept only in memory and never leaves your Mac, the offline promise (*Everything stays local on your Mac, no internet.*), and that passwords from password managers and recognised tokens and keys stay out of history. Below that are an *Open at Login* checkbox (the same login item as in Settings) and two buttons: *Settings…* opens Settings, *Get Started* closes the welcome screen (Esc does too).
- **App icon & Liquid Glass**: the Dock/Finder icon comes from `assets/` via `scripts/build_app_icon.sh` (the Icon Composer document `assets/AppIcon.icon/` is there and is used first, for the macOS 26 Liquid Glass icon; the script only falls back to an `AppIcon.appiconset/` or `icon.icns` when it is missing). The accent colour matches the icon's blue: `assets/AccentColor.colorset` (sRGB #6EB4ED, #7DBFF2 in dark mode) is compiled into the same `Assets.car` and `NSAccentColorName` is set, so default buttons, focus rings, checkboxes, CSV headers and the drop outline (`controlAccentColor`) use it while System Settings › Appearance › Accent colour is Multicolour; a chosen system accent still wins. The card, table window, Settings and About use `mac_ui::glass` (NSGlassEffectView on macOS 26+, frosted fallback before); chips and header buttons use Liquid Glass bezels. The menu bar status item stays a template glyph (system tray).
- **Language**: Settings › Language is System (the macOS preferred language), Nederlands or English. Switching applies at once to the card, the table window, Settings, About, the menu bar menu and command search. An open column picker closes. The texts live side by side in `src/locale/strings.rs` (key, Dutch, English); a unit test checks that each key has both and that every key used in the sources exists. Shared panels follow the same choice: OK, Annuleer / Cancel (Escape answers either), Open, Bewaar / Save, and Formaat: / Format:. The save panel's own Cancel button stays the system button. The drop announcement VoiceOver reads is fixed when the card window is first built, for the life of the process. Left in English on purpose: parser messages from the JSON library, polars error messages, search keywords and file names.
- **About**: *About CopyCraft* (menu bar and `⋯`) shows the name, the bundle version, a short offline promise, the GitHub URL as plain selectable text (CopyCraft does not open it), and two buttons, *Privacy Policy* and *Support*. They open the privacy policy and the support page on GitHub Pages (`https://marcelkoopman.github.io/mac-apps/privacy` and `…/support`, from `docs/privacy.md` and `docs/support.md`; the addresses are in `src/url_policy.rs`) in the default browser through `NSWorkspace`: the browser loads them, CopyCraft makes no connection. When the page cannot be opened, an alert shows the address. The same two buttons are in Settings › Feedback.
- **Settings**: the menu bar *Settings…* (and the card's `⋯` menu) opens a window with five tabs, titled after the open tab (*General*, *Privacy*, …; no app name in the title), picked with the segmented control at the top or ⌘1…⌘5 (its only other keys are ⌘W and ⌘M; while the hotkey field listens every ⌘ chord is recorded instead): *Algemeen* / *General* (hotkey, language, date order, *Open at Login*), *Privacy* (*Keep clipboard history* with, indented 20 pt under it, *Keep history* (*Bewaartermijn*: 5, 15 or 60 min after the last copy, or *Until Cleared*), *Clear sensitive copies after 60 s* and *Ask before clearing*, all three dimmed while history is off and the same settings as in the `⋯` menu, whose notes are their tooltips; then *Use privacy filter*, blur, *Ignored apps*), *Weergave* / *Display* (card theme, pages, *Announce confirmations*, *Symbols*), *Meldingen* / *Notifications* and *Feedback*. The window is 480 × 456 pt, sized to the tallest tab, so it fits a 13" MacBook Air. The last tab is remembered while CopyCraft runs (not stored); switching tabs saves a list being edited and cancels a hotkey being recorded. The settings are the global hotkey (it needs a modifier: a bare key, Option alone or Shift alone is refused with *Need ⌃ or ⌘ (not Option or Shift alone) — try again*), the preferred date order (`dd/mm` or `mm/dd` for ambiguous columns), the language (System, Nederlands or English), *Use privacy filter* (*Gebruik privacyfilter*, off by default: copied content is shown directly; only a stored tick turns the masking on, so an existing install that never touched the box now shows content unmasked too, and a stored choice is kept) and blur on/off for masked content (*Blur masked content*, off by default: a masked card shows a flat cover in its theme's background; only a stored tick turns the blur on, so an existing install that never touched the box now gets the flat cover too), and *Open at Login* (`SMAppService`, off by default; when macOS needs approval it opens System Settings › Login Items). The privacy filter is the switch for the masking itself: with it on, copied content (text card, picture incl. a Base64 picture, table card and table window, dropped items, Decode views) stays blurred until you click it, as described throughout this README. With it off, all of that is shown directly, unblurred and unmasked, with no click to reveal; the blur checkbox, indented 20 pt under it with its note, is then dimmed (it only styles the mask), and without a blur filter the blank-under-a-hint fallback applies only while the privacy filter is on. Switching applies live to the open card and the table window, without a restart: switching it on masks what is shown at once (a click reveals it again), switching it off shows it unmasked. The filter does not touch sensitivity labels in the meta line, Wipe, *Clear History*, lock and retention behaviour, *Allow Screenshots* (`sharingType none` stays) or concealed-pasteboard handling. On the *Privacy* tab, below the blur checkbox, is *Ignored apps*: one line of bundle ids, separated by spaces, commas or semicolons, at most 50. An id is kept only when it is reverse-DNS (it contains a dot, no empty segment, no trailing dot); anything else is dropped, including CopyCraft's own id. *Add Frontmost App* appends the last other app that was in front and is off when there is none. Copies from those apps are never read. Stored in the standard user defaults. The ignore list is settings only; it never holds clipboard text. The *Notifications* tab has four checkboxes, *AI action done*, *PDF recognition done*, *Errors* and *Sensitive copy* (all on by default; a notification never contains your text). On the *Display* tab, first *Kaartthema* / *Card theme*: a pop-up in three groups split by separators: basic *System* (the default), *Light*, *Dark* and *High contrast* (*Hoog contrast*); classic *Night* (*Nacht*, the icon's navy #141E3C), *Sepia*, *Solarized Light*, *Solarized Dark*, *Nord* and *Gruvbox Dark* (*Gruvbox donker*); colourful *Dracula*, *Monokai*, *Catppuccin Mocha*, *Tokyo Night*, *Ocean* (*Oceaan*, CopyCraft's own deep blue with teal) and *Forest* (*Bos*, CopyCraft's own deep green). The well-known themes use their published palettes; a colour that stays under 4.5:1 on its background (mostly comments, and a few reds, pinks and purples) is lightened just enough. A small preview plate beside it shows "Aa" on the selection colour, a JSON key and a number, on the theme's background. The theme colours the card's well: background, text, secondary text, punctuation and line numbers, CSV headers, links, the code colours (keys, strings, numbers, keywords, types, functions, comments) and the diff's added and removed sides. *System* keeps the see-through glass well and the dynamic system colours, so it follows macOS light and dark live (also the menu bar *Appearance* choice), without an observer. Every other theme ignores the macOS mode: the well is opaque in the theme's background (also without Reduce Transparency, so the tested contrast is what you see), the frozen table column takes the same colour, and the card window takes the theme's light or dark appearance so its glass, buttons and the system text selection match. Switching applies at once to an open card, without a restart. The palette lives in `src/card_theme.rs` with tests: text and secondary text, links, accent, every code colour and the diff colours are at least 4.5:1 on the background (WCAG 2), punctuation, line numbers and a link's dimmed path at least 3:1, the diff colours and the text also on the diff rows and the selection; *High contrast* is at least 7:1 for all of them. There is no custom theme editor. The theme also paints the history strip tiles (the theme's background, the tile of the copy on screen on the selection colour, text and a secondary type mark; a compare side keeps its orange frame) and the table window's grid (text, column names in the accent, the header strip, frozen column and plate in the background, odd rows a 6 % step toward the text, and the window's light or dark appearance), also tested at 4.5:1 and 7:1 for *High contrast*; an open table repaints when the theme changes. The welcome screen keeps its own navy, and the table window's toolbar and sidebar, Settings and About stay in the system colours. Then *Show long text in pages* (on by default; long text is shown a page at a time, like `more`: Space, b, g and G page, Esc or q stops), *Announce confirmations* (on by default; VoiceOver says confirmations such as “Copied”, the toast itself stays either way) and *Ask before clearing* (on by default; Clear on the card asks for confirmation first, *Don't ask again* in that dialog turns it off and this checkbox turns it back on). Then *Symbols*: the characters on the card's Symbols button, separated by spaces (commas and semicolons work too; at most 48, each one character, not a word). An empty or invalid list falls back to the standard one (✓ ✗ € → ← • … – — ° ± × ≠ ≤ ≥ ©), which *Restore Defaults* also brings back. The Symbols button opens a small popover with those characters, the ones you used recently (up to 8) first; one click copies that character as plain text only (`public.utf8-plain-text`) and keeps that copy out of the history. The *Feedback* tab has *Suggest a Feature…* and *Report a Problem…* (your mail app opens with a draft; you send it yourself, CopyCraft sends nothing and adds no clipboard, history or files) and the *Privacy Policy* and *Support* buttons, which open those pages in the default browser as in About.
- **Schema / Sample**: JSON → Avro schema (`.avsc`), XML → XSD, XSD → sample XML.
- **Images**: *Info* shows format, size and a data URL. *Text* runs OCR and *QR* reads barcode payloads, both with Apple Vision. After OCR the *Toon tekst* / *Toon afbeelding* toggle (*Show Text* / *Show Image*) switches between the recognised text and the picture. An SVG (copied, opened or dropped) opens as its picture, with its code as the fallback when it cannot be drawn (or points outside itself).
- **Card actions dock**: the actions of the text well (line numbers, reveal, copy, save, preview) sit in a vertical Dock-style icon column on the well's right edge. It is hidden until the mouse is over that edge.
- **Window Size**: the `⋯` menu has Window size / Venstergrootte (Compact 440 pt, Normal 640 pt, Wide 800 pt; Normal by default). The card is never wider than 60% of the screen's usable width and never narrower than Compact; chips, the icon column and the version bar lay out on the chosen width, and the card stays under the menu bar icon. Only the size choice is stored. Dragging the edge to resize is not built.
- **Line numbers**: the numbers button in the dock turns line numbers on or off for every card that shows text or code: plain text, code, Markdown, JSON/YAML, opened files and the text of a picture or PDF (off by default, one choice for all cards, in memory). The numbers are drawn in front of the lines after the syntax colors, so highlighting, search and the Text ▾ jump keep working; tables, pictures, links and the diff have none. The line count and size stay on the meta line; there is no separate count action.
- **Footer statistics**: the meta line under the preview adds counts: a Markdown card shows its headings (`# ` and `## `, outside code fences), a Java card its methods by access level (public, protected, private and package, constructors left out).
- **Image versions**: the *Image ▾* chip (on a copied picture in history and on a dropped or chosen one) has *Resize* ▸ *50%*, *25%*, *Longest side 2048 / 1024 / 512* and *Custom…* (a width, `1200` or `w 1200`, or a height, `h 800`), then *Rotate 90° Left / right*, *Flip Horizontal / vertical*, *Remove Metadata* and *Grayscale*, and *Undo Image Step* / *Redo Image Step*, always there and dimmed when there is no version to go to (*Text ▾* does the same with *Undo Text Step* / *Redo Text Step*; the window's *Table ▾* shows its steps dimmed while it has none yet, instead of beeping); they are in the command search too (undo and redo only when they can run). Resize keeps the proportions and never makes a picture larger: a size it already fits says "Already that size or smaller" in the meta line and makes no version. The picture is read upright (its EXIF orientation applied) with ImageIO, and every version is a PNG without metadata (no EXIF, GPS or camera data); *Remove Metadata* makes such a copy without changing a pixel. Each step is a version of the entry, as for a table: the original, then one per step, at most 20, with the same version capsule (*v2/3*, undo ↶, redo ↷, ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z) in the picture view and the step in the meta line with the version's size (*PNG  1024×768  0.21 MB  ·  Resized to 1024×768*). Only the steps are kept for every version; a version is worked out from the original by replaying them, on a background thread (the spinner shows when slow; a newer step cancels the one running), and the one shown and the two shown before it are kept as pictures, zeroized when they are dropped and with their entry (Wipe, lock, retention, and *Clear History* when the entry is not pinned). *Info*, *Text* and *QR* read the version shown (scanned once it is there, and kept with it). *Copy* puts the version shown on the pasteboard as PNG data (`public.png`) and into history as a new copy. *Save* has a *Format* popup under the panel: *PNG*, *JPEG — high (0.9)*, *JPEG — medium (0.7)* and *HEIC*, all written upright without metadata, and for the original also the original *as it is* (byte for byte, metadata included), which is picked first; for a step's version the source's format is picked first (JPEG, HEIC, else PNG). Switching it switches the name's one extension. The picture stays revealed through its versions.
- **Text ▾**: on every text and code card, including an opened or dropped file (not on a table, a link or a picture), the *Text ▾* chip (*Tekst ▾*; formerly *Text ▾*) has three sections, with headers on macOS 14 and later (separators before that): *Lines* (*Regels*), *Convert* (*Omzetten*: case style, encoding, Clean URL, Format JSON) and *Analysis* (*Analyse*: Complexity and the AGENTS.md steps), then undo and redo. The *Lines* section has Unix-style line tools, worked out in Rust (no subprocess). Line count and size stay on the meta line under the preview. *First Lines…* and *Last Lines…* (`head`/`tail`; a prompt asks for N, 10 to start), *Reverse Lines* (`tac`) and *Column…* (`awk '{print $N}'`; the separator is detected (tab, comma, semicolon, pipe, else runs of whitespace) and can be changed in a pop-up, the column starts at 1, a live preview shows the first lines, and lines without that column are skipped), and *Filter (regex)…* (`grep`, with *Invert* (`grep -v`) and *Ignore case* (`grep -i`); a live preview shows how many lines match and the first of them with the matches highlighted; an invalid pattern is shown in red instead, never an error dialog; patterns are compiled with size limits and match in linear time). Line endings (LF or CRLF) and a final newline stay as copied. Each step copies its result (concealed when it is sensitive, like *Copy*) and so becomes the newest history entry; the versions are those entries, with the same version capsule as a table (*v2/3*, undo ↶, redo ↷, ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z), which only shows the version: the clipboard is left alone and there is no *Copied* toast; *Copy* puts the version shown on the clipboard. Nothing else is stored: only the entries' ids and the step names, forgotten with *Wipe*. A step that would leave nothing says so in the meta line and makes no version. All this work (the steps, the filter and the live previews of *Column…* and *Filter…*) runs on a background thread, like the card itself: only the result comes back to the main thread, which updates the card and the clipboard, and a result that is stale by then (a newer step or keystroke, another entry, Wipe) is dropped.
- **PDF**: a PDF dropped on the card or chosen with *Kies bestand* shows the PDF itself: its first page, drawn in memory with CoreGraphics, as an image card with the same *Toon tekst* / *Toon afbeelding* toggle, *Info* and image versions as any picture. The image (the PDF) is the default; *Toon tekst* shows the text of its first 100 pages, read with PDFKit on the background worker (PDFKit is touched on the main thread only, through `run_on_main`), with the page count first in the footer (*12 pagina's*, or *Eerste 100 van 340 pagina's*) followed by the usual text stats. The image view turns pages (no extra buttons): **Page Up / Page Down** anywhere on the card, **← / →** while the pointer is over the page, **⌥← / ⌥→** from anywhere, and a **scroll wheel or two-finger swipe** over the page (down or left-to-right content movement goes on). Plain ← / → with the pointer elsewhere keep stepping through history, and ⌘← / ⌘→ are always history. The footer says *pagina 2/12* (or *pagina 2/100 (van 340)*; only the first 100 pages can be turned to; at the first or last page there is a beep). Pages are drawn on first visit on a background worker (the page on screen stays until the next one is ready), cropped and scaled like the first, and kept in memory only (at most 12 besides the first, the ones furthest away are dropped first); they are wiped with the file. The text view still shows all pages and the *AI* chip works on that text, whatever page the picture shows. Image versions made on one page are reset when the page turns. The *AI* chip works on that text (cut at 20,000 characters, with the notice), with the usual preview and *Pas toe op klembord*. A PDF with a password is refused with a note. A PDF without a text layer (a scan) gets its first 10 pages recognised with Apple Vision after the card is up; until that is done the *AI* chip is dimmed (*Wacht tot de tekst in de PDF is herkend*), and also when no text is found (*In deze PDF is geen tekst herkend*). A PDF copied in Finder (*Copy*, then the hotkey) has no such card: it shows its text, and a scan is not read; drop it instead. Nothing is written to disk or cached, nothing is logged, and the bytes, text and rendered pages are zeroized like other cards (PDFKit and Foundation keep their own copies while a read runs; those are released when it ends).
- **Word (.docx)**: a `.docx` dropped on the card or chosen with *Kies bestand* shows the **first page as a picture** (drawn by the system's Quick Look thumbnailer, in memory, up to 10 s; only that first page, not the whole document) with the same *Toon tekst* / *Toon afbeelding* toggle, zoom, *Info* and AI chip as a PDF page: *Tekst* is the document's text (paragraphs, tabs, line breaks; table cells separated by a tab, rows by a line). When Quick Look has no page (it fails or times out) and for a `.docx` copied as a file in Finder, the card is the text. Only `word/document.xml` is read: headers, footnotes, comments, text boxes' fallback copies and tracked deletions are left out. All offline and in memory: the zip is never extracted to disk, CopyCraft writes no thumbnail or cache file and never logs content (the system's own Quick Look thumbnail service may keep its own cache outside the app). Memory: the file bytes, the inflated `document.xml` and the extracted text are each one zeroizing buffer of exactly the (checked) declared size, filled in place and never grown, so no reallocation leaves an un-wiped copy; the PNG of the first page is copied once, at its exact size, into a zeroizing buffer. Not covered: `flate2`'s internal inflate window (32 KiB of the document's last bytes, freed without wiping), and the `NSData`, bitmap and `CGImage` objects of the Quick Look picture, which are system objects freed without wiping (the allocator zeroing mentioned under *Wipe* applies to CopyCraft's own heap only). These are tested for the invariants (exact sizes, capacity never exceeded), not for freed memory. Limits: file up to 32 MB, `document.xml` up to 32 MB unpacked, at most 10,000 zip entries, text up to 8 MB; a password-protected document, zip64 or another unsupported zip, or a bomb gets a note instead. The zip directory is read by hand and inflated with `flate2` (pure-Rust backend, already in the tree through polars); no zip crate. The picture comes from `objc2-quick-look-thumbnailing` (QuickLookThumbnailing, macOS 10.15+; `mac_ui::quicklook`, never on the main thread).
- **Clean URL**: *Text ▾ › Clean URL…* (*Tekst ▾ › Schoon URL op…*) on a text or code card with a link that holds a tracking parameter, and the same as a chip on a link card (which has no *Text ▾*). It removes `utm_*` (any `utm_` name), `fbclid`, `gclid`, `dclid`, `msclkid`, `mc_cid`, `mc_eid`, `igshid`, `si`, `ref_src`, `_hsenc` and `_hsmi` (names compared after `%xx` decoding, ASCII case ignored) from every `http(s)://` URL in the text (found as Visit finds them, `url_policy`; trailing `.`, `,`, `)` and the like belong to the sentence). The other parameters keep their order and spelling, the fragment (`#…`) and the text around the URLs stay as copied, and a query left empty loses its `?`. A dialog previews how many parameters go and the first cleaned URLs; *Apply to Clipboard* (*Pas toe op klembord*) makes it a new version like any Text ▾ step (⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z and the version capsule, also on the link card). Pure string work (`text_ops::url_clean`): nothing is fetched, no redirect is followed and no short link is expanded; nothing is logged. Without a tracking parameter the item and the chip are not there (texts over 4 MB are not scanned for them).
- **Format JSON**: *Text ▾ › Format JSON…* (*Tekst ▾ › Maak JSON op…*) on a JSON card. *Format* (*Opmaken*) lays the copy out with a 2-space indent, *Compress* (*Comprimeren*) puts it on one line, and *Sort keys* (*Sleutels sorteren*) orders the keys of every object, nested ones too (byte order; arrays keep their order). The pop-up starts on *Compress* for JSON over several lines and on *Format* for one line. It uses the JSON reading the app already has (`serde_json`, keys in their copied order, the JSON diff's key sort; `text_ops::json_style`): no parser of its own. JSON with a mistake (a *JSON · Invalid* card) shows where it breaks in red (*Line 4, column 1: trailing comma*) and is not changed. So that no data is lost, a key that is there twice in one object or a number `serde_json` cannot write back exactly (an integer beyond 64 bits, more than 15 significant digits) refuses the step with a note. Other spellings of the same value come back as `serde_json` writes them (`1.50` → `1.5`, `1E3` → `1000.0`, `\u00e9` → `é`, `\/` → `/`); a final newline stays. Preview, *Apply to Clipboard* and ⌘Z work like Clean URL.
- **Case Style**: *Text ▾ › Case Style ›* (*Tekst ▾ › Hoofdletterstijl ›*) on every text and code card: camelCase, PascalCase, snake_case, kebab-case, CONSTANT_CASE, UPPERCASE (*HOOFDLETTERS*) and lowercase (*kleine letters*). Each line is one name. Words split at `_`, `-`, spaces and any other character that is not a letter or digit (those go: `Hello, world!` becomes `helloWorld`), at a capital after a lowercase letter or digit, before the last capital of a run that a lowercase letter follows (`HTTPServer` → `httpServer`, `http_server`) and after digits that a letter follows (`md5sum` → `md5_sum`). Digits stay with the word before them: `userID2` is `user` + `ID2`, so `user_id2` and `userId2` (and back). UPPERCASE and lowercase only change the letters. Space around a line, its line ending, empty lines and lines without letters or digits stay as they are. The submenu item opens a dialog with that style picked (it can be changed there), how many lines change and the first of them; *Apply to Clipboard* and ⌘Z as above (`text_ops::case_style`).
- **Encoding**: *Text ▾ › Encoding ›* (*Tekst ▾ › Codering ›*) on every text and code card, the four encodes above a separator and the four decodes below it: *Encode as Base64* / *Decode Base64* (RFC 4648 standard alphabet, with `=` padding), *Encode as Base64 (URL-Safe)* / *Decode Base64 (URL-Safe)* (`-` and `_`, encoded without padding as in JWTs), *Encode for URL* / *Decode URL* (percent-encoding per RFC 3986: everything but `A–Z a–z 0–9 - . _ ~` becomes `%XX` of its UTF-8 bytes; `+` stays a plus) and *Encode as Hex* / *Decode Hex* (lowercase, two digits a byte). NL: *Codeer als Base64*, *Decodeer Base64*, *… (URL-veilig)*, *Codeer voor URL*, *Decodeer URL*, *Codeer als hex*, *Decodeer hex*. The whole text is one value: encoding takes its UTF-8 bytes exactly as copied (a final newline included); decoding ignores whitespace around and inside the code (wrapped Base64, spaced hex, one leading `0x`) and accepts Base64 with or without padding. The result is a new version like every Text ▾ step: on the card and the clipboard, back with ⌘Z. Input that is not that code says *This isn't valid Base64* (*Dit is geen geldige Base64*, and so on) in the meta line, and decoded bytes that are not UTF-8 say *The decoded bytes aren't text*; neither shows or logs the text, and no version is made. Base64 comes from the `base64` crate already used by the app; percent and hex encoding are a few lines of Rust (`text_ops::encoding`). Below it, *Text ▾ › Hash ›* (*Tekst ▾ › Hash ›*), a submenu of its own under *Convert*, has *SHA-256 Hash*, *SHA-1 Hash (Not for Security)* and *MD5 Hash (Not for Security)* (NL *SHA-256-hash*, *SHA-1-hash (niet voor beveiliging)*, *MD5-hash (niet voor beveiliging)*): the hash of the text's UTF-8 bytes exactly as copied, as lowercase hex, made a new version in the same way. MD5 and SHA-1 are broken, hence the label; use SHA-256 for anything that matters. The hash of a sensitive card (any sensitivity label, or a long copy still being checked) is put on the clipboard concealed and stays out of history, with no version: the meta line says *Hash copied, not in history (the text is sensitive)*, because a hash can be checked against a guess. On an opened file the hash is a version of the file card, as for every step there. The hashes come from the RustCrypto crates `sha2`, `sha1` and `md-5` (pure Rust, MIT OR Apache-2.0, no default features; `zeroize` wipes the hasher state), offline like the rest; neither the text nor a hash is ever logged (`text_ops::hash`).
- **New**: the card's `⋯ › New ›` (*Nieuw ›*) makes a value on the spot: *UUID v4* (random), *UUID v7* (time-ordered, RFC 9562), *Unix Time (Seconds)*, *Unix Time (Milliseconds)* and *ISO 8601 Now* (local time with its offset, `2026-10-22T14:03:05+02:00`). The value goes on the clipboard and onto a new card, also with history off; it is also in command search (*uuid*, *epoch*, *iso*). No crate: the random bits come from the system's `NSUUID`, the dates from the decoders' calendar arithmetic (`src/generate.rs`, with unit tests). Values are never logged.
- **Apple Intelligence**: the *AI* chip (✨ `sparkles`, left of *Text ▾*; dimmed with the reason as tooltip when unavailable) offers only what fits the card, at most four entries: **prose** (text, plain, Markdown, and the recognized text of a scanned PDF): *Summarize*, *Rewrite* ▸ *Make Shorter* / *Make More Formal*, *Fix Spelling and Grammar* and *Translate NL↔EN* (every action answers in the language of the text; *Translate* turns Dutch into English and anything else into Dutch) (*Vat samen*, *Herschrijf* ▸ *Maak korter* / *Maak formeler*, *Verbeter spelling en grammatica*, *Vertaal NL↔EN*); **code** (Rust, Java, Python): *Explain What It Does* and *Suggest a Doc Comment* (*Leg uit wat het doet*, *Stel commentaarregel voor*). Code over 5,000 characters, or with more than one function, gets a submenu per action: *Whole text* (only when it is short enough) and the functions by name (up to 30; more are mentioned, not listed), and only the chosen function (with its signature and the doc comment above it) goes to the model, as a part: *Apply to Clipboard* then only copies. A function itself over 5,000 characters is not sent: the preview names the limit. Functions are found lexically (Rust by braces, skipping comments and strings; Java methods; Python `def` by indentation), so odd code can be split wrong; a failed run logs only its kind (`failed`, `too-long`, `guardrail`, `language`, `refusal`); **JSON and YAML**: *Explain the Structure* (*Leg de structuur uit*); **agent instructions** (AGENTS.md and the like): *Summarize* and *Find Unclear Points and Conflicts* (*Zoek onduidelijkheden en conflicten*). Links, XML, HTML, SVG, TOML, tables (CSV, TSV, the table window) and pictures have no AI chip and no actions (logs, regular expressions and shell commands are not recognized as card types, so they get the prose menu as plain text); each has one short, concrete instruction for the model (one task, no added facts, answering in the language of the text (also *Explain What It Does* and *Explain the Structure*)) and the card text is sent as data in its own delimited section (markers inside the text are defused), the model has no tools, files or commands and nothing is logged or stored. The text goes to the model whole, by pointer and byte length (not cut, not NUL-terminated; only the 20,000-character limit below applies), at a low temperature, run by the on-device Apple Foundation Models (crate `mac-ai`: a small Swift bridge, compiled by `build.rs` with `swiftc` and linked into the binary; all `unsafe` of the feature is in that crate). Nothing runs on its own: only a click on one of these items. They need macOS 26, Apple silicon and Apple Intelligence switched on in System Settings; otherwise they are dimmed and say why (tooltip and a last dimmed line). The work runs on a background thread; the result is a preview over the card with *Cancel* (Esc, also stops the model) and *Apply to Clipboard* (*Pas toe op klembord*, Return), which makes it a new Text ▾ version like any other step (⌘Z goes back). Until then nothing is on the clipboard or in history, and closing the preview, the card or Wipe drops the result. The preview has a *Hide* (*Verberg*) button: the card goes to the background and the run and preview stay; while it is hidden the menu bar icon shows … (running) and then ✓ (result ready) or ⚠ (the model failed or refused; the preview has the reason), both read out by VoiceOver, and a click on the icon (or the hotkey) brings the card back with the run or preview intact. The icon and the hotkey hide the card as usual, and with a run or preview that does not end it (also on a PDF card): the next click shows the same card again (not a new card for a newer copy) with the run or result intact. Only *Cancel*, Esc or *Apply* end it. Text over 20,000 characters is cut, with a notice on the preview that *Apply to Clipboard* then only copies: the result covers just the start, so it never replaces the card's text and makes no version (the same goes when a new copy has replaced the card meanwhile). Table cards (CSV, TSV and the like) and the table window have no AI chip or actions. At launch CopyCraft checks in the background whether Apple Intelligence works; when it does not it shows one warning per session (a Dutch or English alert) with the reason and what to do: macOS before 26, no Apple silicon, an unsupported Mac, Apple Intelligence switched off (*Zet Apple Intelligence aan in Systeeminstellingen*), the model still downloading or preparing, a build made without `swiftc` (no Swift bridge), or an unknown status. The *AI* chip shows the same reason as its tooltip while dimmed, and *⋯ › Controleer AI* (*Check AI*) asks again and shows the result, also when everything works; only the status is involved, nothing about your content. The model runs on the Mac: no network and no extra entitlement; text, prompts and results are never logged (a failure logs only its kind) and nothing is cached on disk.
- **Background work**: the card (detection, sensitivity aside, and every format: JSON, XML, Markdown, code, HTML, the dataframe views), the table window's grid, the conversion behind *Copy* (and the table window's Copy), and reading a dropped or chosen file run on a background thread. The main thread waits at most 30 ms, so a quick result looks exactly as before; a slower card shows *Working…* (the spinner after a moment) and is replaced when it is ready, if the card still shows that item and view. Each kind of job has a sequence number: a result overtaken by a newer job is dropped. Only AppKit and the clipboard write stay on the main thread. Table steps, image steps, saves, the diff and Text ▾ already ran in the background.
- **Look and motion**: one set of spacing, corner, type and timing constants (`mac_ui::style`) for the card, footer, Settings, About and the toast. Windows sit on Liquid Glass (macOS 26), vibrancy before that, and an opaque background with Reduce Transparency. The card fades in (150 ms) and out (100 ms); its window never moves or resizes for that, and a "Working…" placeholder keeps the size of the card before it; a new page or filter preview crossfades (120 ms). Reduce Motion keeps the fades. After a copy a small toast with a green check says *Gekopieerd* / *Copied* for about 1.2 s; VoiceOver announces it at normal priority unless Settings › *Announce confirmations* (*Bevestigingen voorlezen*) is off, which keeps the toast. *Wis* / *Clear* on the card first asks *Wissen?* / *Clear?* (*Annuleer* is the default and answers Esc); *Niet meer vragen* / *Don't ask again* turns that off, and Settings › *Ask before clearing* turns it back on. Turning Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast on or off restyles the open card, Settings, About and the toast at once, without a relaunch. At most four chips show on the card (*Text ▾* always); the rest are under `⋯`. Chips and menu items carry SF Symbols, plain glyphs rather than the `.circle` variants (*Info* `info`, *Pause* `pause`, *Remove* `minus`; *Original* `arrow.uturn.backward`, so it no longer looks like *Image ▾*'s `photo`; *Decode* `chevron.left.forwardslash.chevron.right`; *Swap* `rectangle.2.swap`; *Text ▾* `textformat`); one symbol per action (*About* `app`, not *Info*'s; *Check AGENTS.md* `checkmark.seal`, not *Choose Columns…*'s `checklist`). In a submenu the icon sits on its parent row (*Case ▸*, *Encoding ▸*, *Hash ▸*, `⋯` › *New ▸* `plus`), not on the rows inside; *Image ▾* has no icons at all, its *Undo* and *Redo* included. An unknown name leaves the plain title. *Swap* is always under `⋯`, never a chip. Code and tables use SF Mono.
- **Paged long text**: with Settings › *Show long text in pages* on (the default), *Show all* on a text of more than 200 lines shows it one page at a time, like `more`: Space is the next page, b the previous, g and G the first and last, Esc or q shows the whole text again. The last line of the well says *--More-- page 3 of 12 (25%)*. The pages follow the card's height; after a resize the first line shown stays in view. The line index is worked out on the show-all thread, so paging only slices the text. A filtered result is a new entry and pages the same way.
- **Links**: when the clipboard (a drop or a history entry) is one `http`/`https` link and nothing else, the card is titled *Link* (*YouTube* for a video) and shows the URL as plain text, selectable; *Copy* copies it. Nothing is fetched: no page title, preview image or thumbnail. *Visit* opens only `http`/`https` links in your default browser (through `NSWorkspace`), with `user:password@` stripped; the browser loads the page, not CopyCraft.
- **Copy / Save**: copy the current view back to the clipboard, or save it through a save panel. The file extension follows the content; the suggested name is the chosen or dropped file's name without its own extension (*people.tsv* → *people.csv*), else *clipboard*, with that one extension.
- **Choose File**: show a local text file (up to 8 MB) on the card instead of the clipboard. A chosen or dropped file (a PDF too) is the card's last content: hiding the card (icon, hotkey, Esc) keeps it, and the next click shows the same card again, with its versions. It gives way only when new clipboard content comes in from another app (or *Clipboard* in `⋯`).
- **Drop**: drop one text file or text (up to 8 MB), one picture (up to 32 MB), one PDF (up to 32 MB, see *PDF*) or one Word document (`.docx`, up to 32 MB, see *Word (.docx)*; MB is 1,000,000 bytes: 8,000,000 and 32,000,000 bytes), on the card to show it the same way. A picture can be an image file from Finder (PNG, JPEG, HEIC, GIF, WebP, TIFF, BMP, … anything macOS can draw), an image dragged from Safari or Preview, or a photo from Photos (a promised file, written to a temporary folder that is deleted once read). It gets the image card with *Info*, *Text* and *QR*, blurred like a copied image, and *Save* writes it in its own format (or PNG, JPEG or HEIC, see *Image versions*). A drop goes into history like a copy, without changing the clipboard. Several files, folders and other files are refused. The card stays open while you work in another app, so open it, then drag from Finder, Safari, Preview, Photos or Notes onto it.
- **History**: the last 20 unpinned copies and drops (text and images), plus up to 20 pins, kept in memory only (a text over 5 MB or a picture over 20 MB is not kept; over 200 MB in all, the oldest unpinned entries go first), and by default forgotten 15 minutes after the last copy (*Keep History 5 / 15 / 60 Min After the Last Copy* or *Until Cleared* in `⋯`, stored in the user defaults). Whatever that setting, history is always forgotten when the screen locks, the Mac goes to sleep (or its displays do) or you switch to another user, pins included. A pin skips both the 20-copy cap and the 5 / 15 / 60 minute timer. *Clear History* forgets only unpinned copies and leaves pins. *Clear Pins* forgets only pins. *Remove from History* deletes the entry on screen. Each card of the history strip has a ✕ of 20 pt in its top-right corner (shown on the card on screen and the one under the pointer; VoiceOver reads *Remove from History* / *Verwijder uit geschiedenis*): it removes only that copy from the in-memory history, and ⌫ does the same for the card on screen (not while the search field is open). In VoiceOver each strip card also has the actions *Verwijder uit geschiedenis* / *Remove from History* and, on the cards not on screen, *Vergelijk met getoonde* / *Compare with Shown* (VO-⌘-Space). A strip card's tooltip names its type and how long ago it was copied (*JSON · 2 min geleden gekopieerd* / *JSON · copied 2 min ago*), never its content. The clipboard is not touched. The card then shows the next copy, or the empty-card hint when history is empty. ⌘Z right after puts the copy back at the same place (only the last one removed; a new copy, Wipe, lock or another action on the card lets it go, zeroized). *Wipe*, quit, lock, sleep and switching user forget pins as well as everything else. A pinned row is marked with *•* in front of its type and size in the card's *History* list and in the menu bar *History* submenu; that mark is not part of the copied text, and those rows never show the copied sentence. Copying a pinned item again moves it to the front and keeps the pin, its id and its cards. Copying the newest entry again changes nothing. Copying an older unpinned item again replaces it with a new entry. A 21st pin does nothing and the meta line says the pin list is full. Step through them with the **history strip**: a row of small cards along the bottom of the card, one per copy, newest at the left, horizontally scrollable, the copy on screen outlined in the accent colour (a click on a card shows that copy; nothing is copied). ⌘-click (or ⇧-click) a second card to compare it with the copy on screen in the *Diff* view: the older of the two is A (before), the newer B (after); both cards get an orange frame and `A ·` / `B ·` before their type mark (VoiceOver: *compared, side A* / *vergelijking, kant A*; the hint says *⌘-click to compare with the copy on screen*). Both must be text. Esc or a plain click on a card ends the comparison. A right-click (or ⌃-click) on a card opens its menu: *Pin* / *Unpin*, *Add to Diff* (or *Compare with A* / *Remove from Diff*; dimmed for a picture), a separator, and *Remove from History*, for that card's copy, not only the one on screen; `⋯`, the ✕ and the chips keep the same actions. Until you have compared this way once, a small grey line under the strip says *⌘-klik op een tegel om te vergelijken* / *⌘-click a tile to compare* (the card is 16 pt taller for it). After the first ⌘/⇧-click compare it goes blank and does not come back; that is stored in the user defaults (`CopycraftStripCompareUsed`), and the row itself goes the next time the card opens, so the card does not shrink under the pointer. A card is 138 × 78 pt (two to five show whole, depending on the card width) and has the type mark (`{}`, `md`, `img`, a pin for a pinned copy) and the first three lines of the text (at most 20 characters each), or a small picture (pictures over 3 MB show only their type mark). The strip shows with two or more copies and is turned off and on with ⌘Y or `⋯ › Hide/Show History Strip`; the choice is kept in memory only. It follows the privacy filter: while the filter is on, every card except a revealed one on screen shows its type mark and dots instead of content, and the pictures are not drawn. The text and the few decoded pictures live in memory only, are wiped with the card, and nothing is logged. The arrow keys still step (← newer, → older; the newest is first, as in the *History* submenu; ⌘← / ⌘→ work while typing, see *Usage*), or use the menu bar *History* submenu. The old ‹ 1 / N › buttons are gone. Each copy's format is worked out once. Its card, and a picture's scan (info, recognized text, QR payload), are kept with the history entry while it is shown or next to the one shown, so stepping back and forth does not redo them; further away they are zeroized and worked out again when needed, and they never outlive the entry (an unpinned entry falling off the end, *Remove from History*, *Clear History*, *Clear Pins*, *Wipe*, lock, sleep or switching user). A history picture goes back on the pasteboard as data in its own type (JPEG stays JPEG) plus a PNG copy for apps that only paste PNG (TIFF if that conversion fails), never as a temporary file; files an older version left in the temporary folder (`copycraft-history-*`) are removed at launch. Stepping through history (a click on a strip card, ← / →, ⌘← / ⌘→ from search) only shows the entry: nothing is written to the clipboard and there is no *Copied* toast. The card keeps showing that entry through polls and background refreshes until another app copies something new; *Copy* puts the entry shown on the clipboard as the newest copy. Opening the card again starts at the clipboard's item. Everything CopyCraft writes to the pasteboard carries its own type `nl.marcelkoopman.copycraft.self`, so its own writes are not taken for a new copy; *Copy*, *Format* on a link and a history entry chosen from the menu are recorded as the newest copy when they are written.
- **Diff**: compare two text copies. In `⋯` › *History* hold ⌥: each row turns into its diff action (an ⌥ alternate, *Add to Diff: <row>*), which says *Compare with A* once one is chosen and *Remove from Diff* while it is chosen. A line sets *Clear History* and *Clear Pins* apart at the bottom of that submenu. A chosen row is marked *A* or *B* in front of its type and size. The copy on screen has the same action on `⋯` itself, and *Swap* appears there once two are chosen (A on the left, B on the right). A third pick replaces the oldest. Pictures stay disabled. Command search has the same actions. The card then shows a *Diff* chip. The well is A on the left and B on the right; a run of unchanged lines folds to *···*. The meta line says *+N / −M lines*, or *No differences*. JSON objects and arrays are compared after pretty-printing with sorted keys, so key order alone is not a difference; when that leaves them equal, the well still shows each copy as it was. A side over 1 MB or 20,000 lines says *Too large to compare*. While it runs the well says *Comparing…*. Copy and Save take the unified `+` / `-` lines (or *No differences* / *Too large to compare*). The comparison is not a history entry. It stays until Wipe, lock or a cleared history, is masked like other content, and carries the labels of both sides. Nothing is stored on disk.

## Decoders

Text that decodes gets exactly one extra chip, named after what it is. When several fit, the first of JWT > UUID > Unix time > ISO 8601 > URL > Hash > Base64 wins. The chip is tried on plain text, prose and links, and on any copy that is a single token without white space. Detection is string work only (no network, no new rights) and runs with the other chips, off the main thread for a large copy. The decoded view is blurred until clicked and keeps the copy's sensitivity labels (a decoded JWT still says *credential*), plus any labels of what decoding reveals. Labels in the view follow Settings › Language (System, Nederlands or English).

- **JWT** (`header.payload.signature`, header JSON with `alg`): *Signature not verified* / *Handtekening niet gecontroleerd* first (the signature is never checked), then `iat`, `nbf` and `exp` as dates in local time and UTC (Settings date order) with a status: *expired (4 days ago)* / *verlopen (4 dagen geleden)*, *valid for 30 minutes* / *nog 30 minuten geldig* (minutes up to two hours, then hours, days, years), *not valid yet (in 2 hours)* / *nog niet geldig (over 2 uur)*; then header and payload as pretty JSON in the JSON card's colors.
- **UUID**: canonical `8-4-4-4-12` hex, also in braces (`{…}`) or after `urn:uuid:`: the lowercase form, version (*Version 4 (random)* / *Versie 4 (willekeurig)*, …) and variant (RFC 9562, NCS, Microsoft, reserved; nil and max UUIDs are named). Version 1 (100 ns since 1582-10-15) and version 7 (Unix milliseconds) also show their timestamp in local time and UTC, and how long ago. 32 hex digits without dashes are not taken for a UUID. A braced GUID also reads as a YAML flow mapping, so it keeps its *To JSON* chip next to *UUID*.
- **Unix time** / **Unix-tijd**: a bare integer of 10 digits (seconds) or 13 digits (milliseconds) from 2001-09-09 up to 2100: the date in local time (with its offset) and UTC in the Settings date order (`dd/mm/yyyy` or `mm/dd/yyyy`, as for tables), relative (*3 days ago* / *3 dagen geleden*, *in 5 minutes* / *over 5 minuten*), and ISO 8601 UTC. Phone-like numbers (`06…`) and other lengths are left alone.
- **ISO 8601**: a date (`2026-10-22`) or a date and time (`T` or a space; seconds and a fraction optional) with an optional zone (`Z`, `+02:00`, `+0200`, `+02`), from 1970 up to 2100: the Unix time in seconds and milliseconds, then the local time and UTC in the Settings date order and how long ago. Without a zone the text does not say which moment it is, so both readings are shown: as local time (with its offset) and as UTC (`src/decode/iso_date.rs`, with unit tests).
- **URL**: offered when the copy has `%xx` escapes or is a full URL with a query. A URL is shown in parts: scheme, host (with port), path, then the query parameters one `key = value` per line, each decoded (`%xx`, and `+` as a space in the query), then the fragment; `user:password@` is left out. A URL encoded as a whole (`https%3A%2F%2F…`) shows the decoded URL and then its parts; other text shows decoded. A page link with a query gets *URL* after *Visit* (*Visit* stays first); a YouTube video link does not.
- **Hash**: hex digits of one case, with letters and digits, recognised by length only: 32 *Probably MD5* / *Waarschijnlijk MD5* (or a UUID without dashes), 40 SHA-1, 56 SHA-224, 64 SHA-256, 96 SHA-384, 128 SHA-512. Never reversed or looked up. CopyCraft does not compute hashes or encode Base64.
- **Base64**: Base64 and Base64URL, with or without padding, and `data:…;base64,` URIs. Only offered when the bytes are readable UTF-8 text (shown as is), JSON (shown pretty) or a picture: PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP or HEIC is recognised by its magic bytes and described as type, size in pixels (read from the header) and byte count, and the picture itself is drawn in the card's well. It is decoded on a background thread (the card describes it until the picture arrives) and drawn the way a dropped picture is, blurred until clicked like any picture; the description stays on the meta line, with the labels of the copy. Its chip clicked again goes back to the text. A picture over 20 MB decoded, or over 50 megapixels where the header says, or one the system cannot read, is not drawn: the description stays (with *Not shown: larger than 20 MB* / *Niet getoond: groter dan 20 MB*). Copy and Save in this view still take the description text. Ordinary words (*hello*, *Test*, *password*) are not Base64: at least 8 characters, one alphabet, padding only where the length needs it, and decoded text must read as text.

## Privacy

- **Offline by design**: CopyCraft never goes online. It makes no network connections and has no networking code or libraries of its own, and the signed app has no network entitlement (only `com.apple.security.app-sandbox` and `com.apple.security.files.user-selected.read-write`), so the App Sandbox refuses a connection as well. Text recognition and barcode reading (Apple Vision) and the blur (CoreImage) run on the device. *Visit* hands a link to your browser, and so do the *Privacy Policy* and *Support* buttons in About and Settings › Feedback (the GitHub Pages addresses in `src/url_policy.rs`): the browser loads the page, not CopyCraft. CI checks this: `scripts/check_copycraft_offline.sh` fails when CopyCraft's dependency tree holds an HTTP, TLS or socket crate (one exception: `socket2`, which polars' tokio runtime always pulls in and CopyCraft never uses) or its sources mention `osascript`, and `apps/copycraft/clippy.toml` makes clippy refuse `std::process::Command`, `std::net` and `std::os::unix::net`.
- **No other rights**: besides the sandbox and files you pick, the signed app has no entitlements: no hardened-runtime exceptions (`com.apple.security.cs.*`) and no Apple Events. *Choose File* and *Save* use the native open and save panels in the app itself (`NSOpenPanel`, `NSSavePanel`), never `osascript`. A test pins the entitlements file, and `scripts/sign_app.sh` and the release workflow check the signed app.
- **Masked content**: with *Use privacy filter* ticked (off by default), the card masks copied content until you click it. By default (blur off) the well is covered by an opaque plate in the card theme's background (the window background with *System*: light grey, or dark grey in Dark Mode, never a black slab), so nothing of the content shows; the plate says *Click to reveal* (*Klik om te tonen*), or *Checking for sensitive data…* while a long copy's labels are still coming. With the privacy filter off there is no plate or any other cover to click, not even for a diff of a sensitive copy; only the well's *Hide* button covers it then, because you asked. With *Blur masked content* / *Gemaskeerde inhoud vervagen* ticked in Settings › Privacy, the card and the table window blur it instead. The blur is light enough to recognise the shape (lines, indentation, a picture's outline) but keeps text unreadable; pictures get a stronger blur than text, since text in a picture can be large.
- **Sensitivity labels**: the meta line flags **credential**, **PII** and **financial** content. A first layer of CopyCraft's own recognizers runs in one linear regex pass with validation afterwards: private-key headers, JWTs, AWS/GitHub/Slack/Stripe/Google/OpenAI/GitLab/npm tokens, IBAN (mod-97), cards (Luhn), email, NL mobile numbers (on one line, not the tail of a decimal such as `10.06`), BSN (elfproef), and labeled or tabular fields such as names and salaries. [leakguard](https://crates.io/crates/leakguard) then looks only for the classes not found yet (URL credentials, Azure/Telegram/Discord tokens, IP and MAC addresses, US SSNs). Both are linear: about 60 ms for a megabyte. CopyCraft only labels content. It never rewrites it. Copies from 32 KB up are checked on a background thread: the card opens right away, its meta line says *Checking…* until the labels arrive, and with the privacy filter on the content cannot be revealed before that (the click beeps). A newer copy cancels a check that is still running. In documentation (Markdown and agent-instruction files) an e-mail address counts as PII only from three different addresses (a contact list): a README with one or two support or author addresses is not flagged, while a phone number, BSN, IBAN, card, labeled field or key in the same text still is. Anywhere else a single e-mail address is PII, on purpose: a copied address is nearly always a person's.
- **Private copies**: a copy another app marks private with the [nspasteboard.org](http://nspasteboard.org) types (`org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType` from a password manager, `TransientType`, `AutoGeneratedType`) is never recorded in history and its text is not read: the card only says *Hidden content*. A copy whose sensitivity labels include *credential* (a token, key or URL password the recognizers know, such as `ghp_…`, `AKIA…`, `xoxb-…` or a private key) is shown on the card but not recorded in history either, and neither is CopyCraft's own copy of one (a *Text ▾* step then says *Copied, not in history (holds a token or key)*) or a dropped file that holds one (`sensitivity::admission`). A long copy (32 KB or more), copied, dropped or pasted, goes into history only once its labels are in (`sensitivity::record_when`). A picture (copied, dropped or pasted) goes into history only after its text recognition: when the recognised text holds a token, key or URL password, the card shows the picture but it stays out of history (`sensitivity::picture_admission`). When recognition fails, is unavailable or takes over 30 s, the picture is recorded as before. The recognised text is never logged. A PDF's first page and a Quick Look preview are recorded at once, without this check. PII and financial data are kept in history (masked when the privacy filter is on). When CopyCraft itself puts text labelled *credential*, *PII* or *financial* on the pasteboard (*Copy*, a history step), it adds `ConcealedType`, so other clipboard managers skip it, and by default it empties the pasteboard again 60 seconds later if nothing else was copied meanwhile (*Clear sensitive copies after 60 s* in `⋯`, stored in the user defaults).
- **Notifications**: local notifications only (UserNotifications; no push, no `aps-environment`, no network entitlement, no Info.plist key), and a notification never holds your text: no clipboard content, no AI result, no file name, no line number. Four kinds, each with a switch in Settings › *Meldingen* / *Notifications* (all on by default): an AI action is done (the text is only the action's name, *AI-actie klaar: Vat samen* / *AI action done: Summarize*; only when the card is hidden or CopyCraft is not the active app; a failed run says nothing; a click opens the card with the AI preview); a PDF's text recognition took longer than about 3 s and the card is hidden; an error you must know (the pin list is full, Apple Intelligence is not available), at most once per kind per session and only when the card is hidden or CopyCraft is in the background; and a copy from another app that may hold a credential (*Mogelijk gevoelige tekst gekopieerd* / *Possibly sensitive text copied*, nothing else; whatever the privacy filter says, and never for a copy another app marked private, which is not read at all). That one comes only from watching the clipboard, so only with *Keep clipboard history* on: not while history is paused, not for a copy from an ignored app, and not for a token you paste (⌘V) or drop on the card. With macOS pasteboard access on Ask or Default, a copy is read only while the card is shown, so the notification comes when the card shows it. At most one notification per 5 seconds. A notification is removed as soon as the card opens. Permission is asked only the first time one is needed: a short Dutch or English dialog explains it, then macOS asks. A *No Notifications* or a macOS refusal is final: nothing is shown and CopyCraft never asks again (macOS Settings › Notifications can still change it, but CopyCraft stays silent after a refusal). Nothing about notifications or their content is logged. The decision is a pure function (`src/notifications.rs`, with unit tests).
- **Feature suggestions and problem reports by mail**: *Idee voorstellen…* / *Suggest a Feature…* and *Probleem melden…* / *Report a Problem…* (in the `⋯` menu and in Settings › *Feedback*) open a small window with a subject, two text fields (*What do you want the app to do?* / *Why is that useful?*, or *What went wrong?* / *What did you expect?*) and a live preview of the mail. *Maak e-mail* / *Create Email* hands a `mailto:` link to mac-apps-support@proton.me to your default mail app (`NSWorkspace`; the subject starts with *Feature voorstel:* or *Probleem:*) and the mail app shows a draft: **nothing is sent automatically, you send the mail yourself**, and CopyCraft makes no network connection for it. The body is only what you typed plus the CopyCraft and macOS version; never clipboard content, history, file names, paths or settings. The typed text is not logged or stored, and the window wipes its fields when it closes. The link is limited to 2000 characters (`MAX_MAILTO_LEN` in `src/mailto.rs`); when the text is longer, or no mail app handles `mailto:` links, CopyCraft says so and offers *Kopieer naar klembord* / *Copy to Clipboard* (the whole mail as plain text, marked as CopyCraft's own write so it is not recorded in history). Opening a `mailto:` link needs no entitlement in the App Sandbox. The link building, encoding and length limit are a pure function with unit tests (`src/mailto.rs`).
- **Polling** (only with *Keep clipboard history* on; off, nothing is polled and only ⌘V reads the pasteboard): macOS has no notification for clipboard changes, so CopyCraft looks at the pasteboard's change count every 3 seconds while the card is closed and every 400 ms while it is open; the frontmost app is only asked when the count changed. Only when the count changes is the copy read, hashed and recorded, unless recording is paused, this is the ignored next copy, or the frontmost app is on the ignore list. Copying the newest entry again changes nothing in history. Pasteboard privacy (macOS 15.4+, see `docs/pasteboard-privacy/FINDINGS.md`): the access setting (*Privacy & Security › Paste from Other Apps*) picks how copies are read, and is looked at again on every look at a new copy and whenever the card opens, so a change in Settings is picked up while CopyCraft runs (and logged). *Allow* (and macOS before 15.4): every copy is read as it comes, as above. *Ask* (or never asked yet): while the card is closed CopyCraft only watches the change count and the types (no alert); the menu bar tooltip says *New copy — open the card to view*. It also names the kind of copy when macOS recognises one: *New copy: Link — open the card to view*. The other kinds are *Number*, *Email address*, *Phone number*, *Calendar event* and *Postal address* (NL *Link*, *Getal*, *E-mailadres*, *Telefoonnummer*, *Kalenderevent*, *Postadres*). The kind comes from `NSPasteboard detectPatternsForPatterns:completionHandler:`, asked once per copy, only under *Ask* or never asked yet (not under *Deny*). It reads no content and shows no alert: CopyCraft learns which patterns match, never the text. The kind is not logged and is forgotten with the next copy (`paste_access::PasteHint`, `macos_pasteboard::hint`). The copy is read when you open the card, in one pass on the main thread that history, the card, the picture and its scan share, so macOS asks at most once per copy. Copies you never open are not read, so they are not in history. The first time, the card first explains the alert (once): choose *Allow* to see the copy, or set CopyCraft to *Always Allow* in Settings. *Deny*: CopyCraft reads no contents at all (only the change count and the types) and the card says *Clipboard access denied in Privacy & Security*. A reading that comes back empty, fails or is denied is not tried again until another copy or other types, and the pasteboard is only read on the main thread (the image scan decodes on its own thread what was fetched before).
- **Pause and ignore-next** (only with history on; greyed out otherwise): *Pause Recording* and *Ignore Next Copy* live in the menu bar menu (both with a check mark), in `⋯` and in command search. They are session flags: off at every start, and not stored in the user defaults. Pause survives lock, sleep and a user switch; ignore-next stays armed too. While paused, a new copy from another app is not read and the menu bar says *Paused*. An ignored copy, while recording is not paused, says *Copy ignored*. Ignore-next skips one such copy, then turns off. A skipped copy stays unread after you resume or unlock, until a newer copy. Concealed, transient and auto-generated copies stay *Hidden content* and do not use up ignore-next. CopyCraft's own writes (*Copy*, a history step) are always read. Pause does not use up ignore-next.
- **Ignored apps** (only with history on; greyed out otherwise): a copy is never read when the frontmost app's bundle id is on the ignore list (Settings, at most 50). The source app is whoever is frontmost when the poller sees a new change count, so it can be the wrong app if you switch inside the poll interval (3 s with the card closed, 400 ms while it is open). macOS has no public pasteboard-writer API. The list applies on the next poll. Copies already in history stay. Removing an app does not read what was skipped. *Add Frontmost App* and *Ignore Copies from the Frontmost App* append the last other app that was frontmost; the Settings window itself is not added.
- **Search**: typing on the card searches actions and the text already held in history, case-insensitive, up to the first 64 KB of each entry. A match only past 64 KB is missed. With the privacy filter on, a history hit is titled with its type and size (and *•* when pinned). With the filter off, the title is one line of the text, trimmed and cut at about 80 characters; a match outside that line still hits. Images do not match their pixels or their recognized text. The menu bar *History* submenu and the `⋯` history rows stay type and size and never show the copied text.
- **Clear All**: the *Clear All* button (*Alles wissen*, a trash icon top right of the card, set apart from the group of the special characters button (`character` symbol), ⋯ and ✕; its tooltip and VoiceOver label say *Clear All* / *Alles wissen*; every icon-only button has a tooltip with the words VoiceOver reads, plus the shortcut where there is one: ✕ says *Close (Esc, ⌃⌘C)*, the well's save button *Save … (⌘S)*) clears history, pins included, plus caches, the card and the pasteboard. It asks first (*Clear all?*) unless *Ask before clearing* is off in Settings › Display. Copied text and image buffers are zeroized when they are dropped (`zeroize`). Beyond that, CopyCraft's own allocator overwrites every block of heap memory with zeros before it is freed, so copies that libraries make on the way (Polars' table columns and the CSV reader's chunks, rendered grids, hash tables) do not linger in freed memory either.
- **Masked again on focus loss**: the card stays open (floating) when another app becomes active, but with the privacy filter on, content you revealed is masked again. It closes with ✕, Esc, the hotkey or a click on the menu bar icon.
- **Out of screen capture**: the card and the table window ask macOS to leave them out of screenshots, screen recordings and screen sharing (`NSWindowSharingNone`). That is a request, not a guarantee: Apple documents the setting as a legacy constant not to be used to hide content from capture ([NSWindow.SharingType.none](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nswindow/sharingtype-swift.enum/none)), and ScreenCaptureKit on macOS 15.4+ is reported to capture such windows anyway; Apple says there is no public API to prevent screen capture ([Developer Forums 792152](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/792152)). So capture is not the protection. By default the privacy filter is off and copied content is plainly visible on the card, so anything that can capture the screen can see it. With *Use privacy filter* on (Settings › Privacy), the mask is the protection: content stays covered until you click, and *Blur masked content* only changes what the cover looks like. For testing, *Allow Screenshots* in the menu bar menu (with a checkmark) lets screenshots, recordings and screen sharing capture the card and the table window (read-only sharing) until it is switched off; it is off at every start and never stored. The privacy filter, its mask and the wipe work the same with it on or off.

## Code complexity

On a Rust, Java or Python card, *Text ▾ › Complexity* (*Tekst ▾ › Complexiteit*) lists every
function or method with its line range and cognitive complexity, highest first, with the
total and average below. 16–25 is shown in orange and marked *high*, above 25 in red and
marked *very high*. Click a row to scroll the card to that function. The scores are worked
out in the background and are an approximation of SonarQube's cognitive complexity (one
lexical pass, no parser): `if`, `else if`/`elif`, `else`, ternaries, `switch`/`match`, loops,
`catch`/`except`, labelled `break`/`continue`, mixed `&&`/`||` (`and`/`or`) sequences and
direct recursion count; nested structures, lambdas and closures add one per level. Only
counts and timings are logged, never code. Texts over 512 KB or 20,000 lines are not scanned and show
*Too large for complexity analysis* instead.

## Agent instructions

`AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `.cursorrules`, `.cursor/rules/*.mdc`, `copilot-instructions.md`
and `GEMINI.md` opened with *Choose File* get the *Agent instructions* card
(*Agent-instructies*). Pasted text gets it only when it clearly reads as instructions:
Markdown with several typical headings (Build, Test, Setup, Code style, Conventions,
Commands, PR instructions) and a shell code block. A README stays Markdown. The footer shows
a rough token estimate (characters / 4). *Text ▾* then offers:

- *Commands from AGENTS.md* (*Commando's uit AGENTS.md*; also on CLAUDE.md and the others): the lines of shell/bash/sh/console blocks and inline commands
  in lists (cargo, npm, pnpm, yarn, make, pytest, go, git, gradle, mvn), in order, each
  once, as a new version on the clipboard (⌘Z goes back for a copied text; an opened file
  is left for the clipboard).
- *Heading Outline* (*Koppenoverzicht*): the headings; pick one to scroll the card there.
- *Check AGENTS.md* (*AGENTS.md controleren*): possible secrets (line and kind only, never the value), a very long
  file (over 500 lines or about 8k tokens), empty sections, duplicate headings, in-file
  `#anchor` links that match no heading (links to other files are not checked) and a missing
  Build or Test section. A section counts only when its heading is exactly a known name
  (case-insensitive, trailing `:` ignored), such as *Build* or *Testing*.

Only counts are logged, never the instructions.

## Logs and diagnostics

CopyCraft logs events only: what happened, counts, sizes, types and error kinds. It never
logs clipboard or history text, file contents, file names or paths, decoder input or output,
regex patterns or table data.

**View the log in Console.** Open Console.app, select your Mac, click Start streaming and
search for `subsystem:nl.marcelkoopman.copycraft`. The categories are
`clipboard`, `table`, `decode`, `settings`, `window`, `login` and `worker`. Include Info and
Debug messages (Action > Include Info Messages / Include Debug Messages) to see everything.

**Export diagnostics.** In the `⋯` menu choose *Export Diagnostics…* (*Diagnose
exporteren…*) and save the `.txt` file. It holds the app version, the macOS version, the
settings (values only) and the log lines of the last 24 hours of the current run, kept in
memory. Attach it to your support request.

## Build and run

From the repo root:

Needs macOS 26 and Xcode 26 (the Swift bridge of the Apple Intelligence actions is compiled by
`crates/mac-ai/build.rs` with `swiftc`; without `swiftc`, or on another OS, the app builds and
reports the actions as unavailable). The binary also says macOS 26 as its minimum: `.cargo/config.toml` sets
`MACOSX_DEPLOYMENT_TARGET=26.0` for every cargo build; CI and the release workflow check that the
release binary's Mach-O `minos` is 26.0 (`scripts/check_minos.sh`). The `unsafe` count is capped by
`scripts/count_unsafe.sh --check` against `scripts/unsafe_baseline.txt` (copycraft 111 blocks, mac-ui 83,
mac-ai 6; the AI, strip, line-number, window-size and notification code added none to copycraft). mac-ui went from
78 to 83 in four steps: +1 in `mac_ui::notify` (the permission settings object handed to the completion block,
79), +1 for the `scrollWheel:` override of `PassiveTextScroll` in `mac_ui::widgets` (⌘ + scroll zooms; the rest goes
to `super` with `msg_send!`, 80) and +2 in `mac_ui::quicklook` (the Word first page: CGImage → PNG, and the thumbnail
request; 82) and +1 for `mac_ui::widgets::set_context_menu` (`setMenu:` for the strip cards' right-click menu, 83). The per-block history is in the comments of `scripts/unsafe_baseline.txt`. PDFs use the `objc2-pdf-kit`
and `objc2-core-graphics` bindings (PDFKit and CoreGraphics, on the main thread only). The Word
first page uses `objc2-quick-look-thumbnailing` (on a background thread).

```bash
cargo run -p copycraft
cargo test -p copycraft
cargo clippy -p copycraft --all-targets -- -D warnings
```

Test data: [`testdata/`](testdata/README.md) has a synthetic input for every scenario
(detection, convert and decode, tables, diffs, sensitivity labels, edge cases, images) with a manifest
of what the card should show. `cargo test -p copycraft testdata_manifest` checks the manifest
against the app; `testdata/generate_large.sh` writes the 1 MB and over-8 MB files.

Local app bundle (needs `cargo-bundle`):

```bash
cd apps/copycraft && cargo bundle --release
```

## App Store readiness

Preparation only: CopyCraft is not submitted to the Mac App Store, and none of this was run through App Store review. What is in the repo:

- **Bundle identifier**: `nl.marcelkoopman.copycraft` (`identifier` in `[package.metadata.bundle]`, `OWN_BUNDLE` in `src/capture.rs` and the log subsystem `diag::SUBSYSTEM`, which must all match; the pasteboard type `nl.marcelkoopman.copycraft.self` already used that prefix). It replaced `com.github.marcelkoopman.mac-apps.copycraft` (used by the GitHub releases up to v0.1.87) before the first Store submission. A new bundle id means a new sandbox container (`~/Library/Containers/<id>/`) and a new defaults domain, so a copy that was installed under the old id starts from the defaults once (language, privacy filter, hotkey, date order, ignored apps, history timer, the welcome screen shows again), macOS asks again for *Paste from Other Apps*, and Open at Login has to be turned on again (the old login item points at the old id; remove it in System Settings › General › Login Items). There is no migration code: a sandboxed app cannot read another id's container. The old container can be deleted by hand. `scripts/check_copycraft_offline.sh` fails when an old id is back in the code.
- **Privacy manifest**: `apps/copycraft/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`, shipped as `Contents/Resources/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` through `resources` in `[package.metadata.bundle]`. It declares no tracking, no tracking domains and no collected data (CopyCraft is offline). Required-reason APIs, from reading the sources and the dependencies:
  - `UserDefaults` **CA92.1**: the app's own settings and appearance, in its own defaults domain (`src/settings.rs`, `mac_ui::theme`).
  - `FileTimestamp` **C617.1**: `std::fs::metadata` (stat) on its own files in the app container (preview files in the temporary directory). **3B52.1**: stat on files you picked with the open panel, dropped on the card or copied from Finder (`src/open_file.rs`, `src/macos_pasteboard.rs`).
  - `SystemBootTime` **35F9.1**: `std::time::Instant` (the monotonic uptime clock on macOS) for elapsed time between events inside the app.
  - Not declared: disk space (no statvfs or volume-capacity calls in CopyCraft; `sysinfo` is in the tree through polars but without its disk feature) and active keyboard APIs (Apple lists the UIKit call; winit's keyboard-layout lookup is not on the list).
  - These are my reading, not an Apple verdict; App Store Connect reports missing declarations on upload.
- **Export compliance**: `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` is `false` in `assets/Info.plist.ext`. CopyCraft has no network code and no encryption of its own; the dependency tree holds hashing for deduplication (std `DefaultHasher`, not cryptographic) and `rand` with `chacha20` as a random number generator (via polars), not used to encrypt anything. `scripts/check_copycraft_offline.sh` keeps TLS and HTTP crates out. The declaration to Apple is the owner's to make; check it before upload.
- **Store build (placeholders, not run yet)**: `apps/copycraft/assets/entitlements.store.plist` is the sandbox entitlements plus `com.apple.application-identifier` (`TEAMID.nl.marcelkoopman.copycraft`) and `com.apple.developer.team-identifier` (`TEAMID`). `scripts/build_store_pkg.sh` (maintainer only, macOS, not in CI) builds the release, bundles it, embeds the provisioning profile as `Contents/embedded.provisionprofile`, fills in the team id, signs with the Apple Distribution identity and those entitlements, and builds a signed `.pkg` with `productbuild --sign` and the Mac Installer Distribution identity. It needs `APPLE_TEAM_ID`, `STORE_APP_IDENTITY`, `STORE_INSTALLER_IDENTITY` and `STORE_PROVISION_PROFILE`, and stops with a clear message when an identity is not in the keychain (`security find-identity`) or the profile is missing or for another team or app.
- **Checks**: `apps/copycraft/tests/privacy_manifest.rs` (runs everywhere); `scripts/check_privacy_manifest.sh` (macOS: `plutil -lint`, the file is in the bundle, the export key is in `Info.plist`), called by `scripts/sign_app.sh`, the release workflow (before and after signing) and CI (source file). To check by hand on a built app: `plutil -lint CopyCraft.app/Contents/Resources/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` and `plutil -p CopyCraft.app/Contents/Info.plist | grep ITSApps`.

Also in the repo: draft review notes for the App Review Information *Notes* field in [`docs/app-review-notes.md`](../../docs/app-review-notes.md), and the privacy policy and support URLs App Store Connect asks for (`https://marcelkoopman.github.io/mac-apps/privacy` and `https://marcelkoopman.github.io/mac-apps/support`, served by GitHub Pages from [`docs/privacy.md`](../../docs/privacy.md) and [`docs/support.md`](../../docs/support.md)).

Still missing for the Store: the Apple Developer account itself, the Apple Distribution and Mac Installer Distribution certificates and the Mac App Store provisioning profile for `nl.marcelkoopman.copycraft` (`scripts/build_store_pkg.sh` is ready for them but has never run), and the App Store Connect record itself: description, screenshots, category, age rating and privacy answers (these must match the manifest), with the review notes pasted in. The sandbox entitlements already exist; whether they suffice for review (for example the global hotkey and pasteboard polling) has not been tested.
